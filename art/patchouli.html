Patchouli

Finished drawing

View original image

One of my strongest motivations back when I started digital drawing was trying to depict Patchy: an apathetic, reclusive magician who spends all her time reading all sorts of books. In my imagination, she should be pale from anemia and rolling her eyes at you because you disturbed her reading. The color palette should also be muted and low-saturation. (Well, that was my original idea; as I progressed, I discovered that highly saturated purple is beautiful.)

I’ve made multiple attempts, but every time I looked at one of them, I was like, “😭 No, this isn’t her. This can’t be called Patchouli.” Recently, after finishing that complex Rumia drawing, I felt more confident in my understanding of color and luminosity, so I tried drawing Patchouli again.

A very deliberate control over the color/gradient mapping is tried here; making things grey at a certain dark range turns out to be a common practice, a very effective way of expressing material relation: e.g., hair: the general purple is supposed to be unrealistic if I smash the purple on, but that right grey portion of dark makes the "color mapping relation as things gets darker" look convincing

Another thing I was trying to do was draw "shape" without caring about its legit shape; this allows me to drastically increase room to play with color and weird, satisfying shapes to occupy the position/ space I want them to. Making things "blurry" also allows you to figure out "what is this" and makes it more fun to stare at(oh hey, a highly saturated lovely pink used as a boundary here)

Many of the line sketches and background elements deliberately lack detail; they’re meant to direct attention toward the main subject. Hopefully, I managed that well enough.

I hope people enjoy this. It’s a piece I put a good amount of effort and love into.

As a pink enjoyer, I consider purple an alternative shift of pink; I love pink <=> purple
